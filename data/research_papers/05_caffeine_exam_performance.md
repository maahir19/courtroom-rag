# Caffeine Intake, Sleep and Exam Performance in Engineering Undergraduates: A Prospective Cohort Study

Authors: V. Rao, N. Deshpande, A. George
Institute: Western Institute of Technology (fictional), 2025

## Abstract

We followed 412 second- and third-year engineering students through one end-semester exam period. Students recorded daily caffeine intake (coffee, tea, energy drinks) and sleep using a mobile app. Exam scores were obtained from the academic office with consent.

Moderate caffeine intake (100–300 mg per day, roughly one to three cups of coffee) was associated with slightly higher scores than no caffeine (mean +2.1 percentage points). High intake (more than 400 mg per day) was associated with lower scores (mean −3.4 percentage points) compared with moderate intake.

## Sleep as the key factor

High caffeine users slept on average 1 hour 12 minutes less per night during the exam period. After adjusting for sleep duration, the negative association between high caffeine and exam scores was no longer significant. This suggests that caffeine harms performance mainly by cutting into sleep, especially when consumed after 16:00.

Students who slept fewer than six hours on the night before an exam scored on average 6.8 percentage points lower than those who slept seven hours or more, regardless of caffeine intake.

## Energy drinks

Energy drink users reported more palpitations, anxiety and "crashes" than coffee or tea drinkers. 18% of energy drink users reported at least one episode of heart palpitations during the exam period.

## Limitations

This was an observational study, so it cannot prove that caffeine causes higher or lower scores. Students who drank more caffeine may also have studied differently or been more stressed. Caffeine amounts were self-reported and estimated from drink types.

## Conclusion

Moderate caffeine is unlikely to harm exam performance and may slightly help, but high intake that reduces sleep is linked to worse results. Protecting sleep before exams appears to matter more than caffeine itself.
