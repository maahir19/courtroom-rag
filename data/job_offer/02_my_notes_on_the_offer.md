# My notes on the Quillstack offer (written after the HR call)

> Synthetic sample notes written for testing Courtroom RAG.

## First reaction

Honestly excited. The interviews were the best I've had — the system design round felt like a real discussion and not a quiz. My would-be manager (Priya) has worked on API gateways at a big company before and said I'd own the rate limiting service within three months. That kind of ownership is what I've been missing.

## Money stuff — need to be careful

The CTC of ₹18.4L looks big but a lot of it is not cash. The joining bonus is one-time and the bonus is "up to" 10%. The ESOP number is just an estimate and the company is only Series A, so it could be worth nothing. If I only count base salary, it's ₹14L.

My current fixed salary is ₹11.5L, so the real fixed hike is about 22%, not 60% like the CTC makes it look.

Bengaluru rent will be higher than Pune. A friend in HSR pays ₹24,000 for a 1BHK shared between two people, so my share would be around ₹12,000–14,000 plus deposit.

## The things that worry me

- It's a startup. If funding dries up, layoffs could happen. They raised Series A in March 2025, so by early 2027 they'll probably need to raise again.
- The joining bonus clawback means I'm kind of locked in for a year.
- Parents are not covered under health insurance. Right now my current company covers my parents, and Dad has a heart condition. A parental top-up would cost me something like ₹25,000–35,000 per year.
- Hybrid with 3 office days — HSR is far from where most of my friends stay.

## The things I like

- Learning. Real backend work in Go, Kubernetes, and actual production on-call. My current job is mostly maintaining a legacy Java monolith.
- Smaller team, so I'd get noticed. Priya said strong performers have been promoted to SE-II within 14 months.
- Quillstack's product is used by a lot of developers; the GitHub repo for their open-source CLI has a big community.

## Gut feeling

Seventy percent leaning towards yes. The thing that would change my mind is the parent insurance issue and if I find out the company is running out of money.
